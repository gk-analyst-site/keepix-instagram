CAPTION:
Ten years on the training pitch will teach you a few things. You learn which drills the keepers hate but need. You learn how quickly a plan falls apart the moment you unpeel a ball bag.

And you learn that the gap between what you drew up and what actually happens on the grass is where most coaching time disappears.

For years I worked around it. Napkins, torn notebook corners, notes scrawled on my phone at 6 a.m. the day of a session. Some coaches I respect deeply still do the same. There's nothing wrong with the instinct — the problem is when the plan can't keep up with you once the cones are out.

That frustration is why KEEPIX exists. Not because we wanted to build a product. Because we wanted to stop losing good sessions to poor preparation — and we suspected we weren't the only ones.

We built it around the way goalkeeper coaching actually works: the progressions, the repetitions, the ability to adapt mid-session when your keeper is struggling with their set position or a crossing drill suddenly clicks and you want to push further.

The napkin was always a workaround. This is what we wished we'd had from the start.
