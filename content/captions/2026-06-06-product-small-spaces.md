CAPTION:
Most keepers don't train at a full-size facility every session. Shared pitches, indoor halls, marked-off corners — that's the reality for a lot of coaches. The question isn't whether you can do proper GK work. It's how you set it up.

KEEPIX is built around that constraint. Because the frame stays exactly where you position it, you can run footwork sequences, angled shot-stopping, and distribution triggers in a corridor of space — without chasing loose equipment or waiting for a ball to come back to you.

What changes in a tight space:
— Angles become your coaching focus, not distance.
— Footwork quality gets exposed faster. There's nowhere to hide sloppy movement.
— Repetition goes up. Less time walking, more time working.

One setup we come back to regularly: two cones, four metres apart, KEEPIX positioned just outside the six-yard box equivalent. The keeper works a side-shuffle across, sets, receives the return, resets. Clean, contained, coachable — and you can run it with one goalkeeper in a corner of a shared session.

Quality doesn't need space. It needs the right structure.
