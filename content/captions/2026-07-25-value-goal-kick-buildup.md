CAPTION:
Most coaches drill the pass. Fewer drill the moment before it — and that's where build-up from the back actually breaks down.

Here are three things we work on with keepers to make them a genuine first option out of goal kicks:

1 — SCAN BEFORE YOU SET
The keeper should be reading the press shape while the ball is still being retrieved. By the time it's placed on the ground, they need a picture: where is pressure coming from, which channel is open, who is available short. Scanning after the whistle is already too late.

2 — BODY SHAPE IS A PASS
Receiving foot slightly open, hips angled away from the heaviest press — this alone gives the keeper an extra half-second because defenders have to recalculate their trigger. Chest pointed square to goal tells the whole pitch "I'm in trouble." One small angle change says the opposite.

3 — KNOW THE FIRST SAFE OPTION BEFORE THE BALL ARRIVES
We ask keepers to identify their safe ball before kickoff. Not a plan — a picture. If pressure arrives, the foot goes there automatically. Under stress, keepers default to what they rehearsed. Make sure what they rehearsed is a good decision.

Work these three in sequence during your next set-piece shape session. You will see the difference inside twenty minutes.
