Most pre-match warm-ups treat the goalkeeper as an afterthought — a few rolling saves while the strikers are stretching. That's not preparation. That's guesswork.

Below is the structure we use. Adapt the timing to your squad, but keep the three phases.

─────────────────────

PHASE 1 — ACTIVATION (0–7 min)
Get the body ready before the ball comes out.

• Dynamic hip openers, lateral shuffles, and high knees across the 6-yard box
• Footwork ladder or cone patterns: side-step, crossover, set-and-recover
• Light resistance band work around the hips if you have it

Goal: elevate heart rate and switch on the stabilisers GKs actually rely on.

─────────────────────

PHASE 2 — HANDLING PROGRESSION (7–15 min)
Start clean, build complexity.

• Low, gentle rolls to the hands — reinforcing the set position and soft catch
• Progression to waist-height, then chest, then crosses from the corner flag
• Finish with two or three sharper driven shots to simulate match pace

Goal: build rhythm and confidence in the hands. The GK should feel the ball, not react to it.

─────────────────────

PHASE 3 — 1v1 PRIMERS (15–20 min)
The last thing in the warm-up should mirror the hardest thing in the match.

• Two or three 1v1 scenarios from the D: force the decision, not just the save
• One or two distribution reps — throw or kick, whatever suits your system
• Then stop. Leave them sharp, not fatigued.

─────────────────────

Twenty minutes is enough. The mistake is trying to fit everything in — the goal is to arrive at kick-off ready, not worn out.
