HOOK (first line of caption): Low shot or high shot — your entry should never look the same.

CAPTION:
One of the most overlooked details in goalkeeper coaching: the stance entry before a save.

For low shots, you want to collapse into the ground as efficiently as possible. Weight forward, hips dropping early, lead hand getting behind the ball — not diving over it. The recovery line is lateral, back to your feet, set position re-established quickly. Scramble saves happen when the entry was late or upright.

For high shots, the mechanics flip. You need vertical lift, so the push-off angle changes — drive through the balls of your feet, keep your near-side hip open. Landing matters here too: get your feet under you on the way down. A keeper who lands flat is already behind on the next action.

Where it gets interesting is the reading phase — before either entry happens. Shot height is telegraphed earlier than most keepers think. Striker's shoulder angle, plant foot position, and first contact zone on the ball all give you half a second of information. Train your keepers to read those cues, not just react to ball flight.

Three things to work into your next session:
① Low-shot entries from a compressed set position — isolate the collapse mechanics
② High-shot entries with a deliberate push-off drill, no ball at first
③ Mixed-height feed sets so the keeper must read and commit — no cueing from you

The difference between a clean save and a scramble often lives in the three frames before the keeper moves.
