CAPTION:
Not 25 minutes of every drill in your notebook. Eight minutes, a clear sequence, and the keeper arrives at kick-off ready — not exhausted.

The structure we use:

Minutes 1-2: Dynamic movement. Not static stretching. High knees, lateral shuffles, hip openers. Get the feet alive before touching a ball.

Minutes 3-4: Handling under light service. Low rolls, medium height throws, no power yet. Hands getting used to the ball, eyes calibrating.

Minutes 5-6: Shot-stopping from close range, building intensity. Two or three saves each side. Feet moving on every rep, not just reaching.

Minutes 7-8: One high ball sequence (cross or lofted serve), one shot from distance at full stretch. Something that requires real decision-making. End warm, end sharp.

That's it. The keeper is ready. Anything beyond eight minutes is for the coach's nerves, not the keeper's preparation.
