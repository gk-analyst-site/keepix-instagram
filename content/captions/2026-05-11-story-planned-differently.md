HOOK (first line of caption): We used to plan sessions the same way every week. Then we stopped.

CAPTION:
The format was always the same: warm-up, technical block, small-sided game, cool-down. Clean. Logical. Predictable.

The keepers got comfortable. Too comfortable. They started arriving knowing roughly what was coming. The sharp edge — that moment of genuine uncertainty where a keeper has to think rather than react — had gone out of the sessions.

We changed one thing: the order.

Same drills. Same total volume. But we moved the technical block to the end of sessions twice a week, after the keepers were tired. We moved the crossing work to the start once a week, before they'd warmed into their rhythm.

Two things happened. The keepers made more mistakes — which meant more real coaching moments. And over the following weeks, their late-game concentration improved. Not because we'd added harder drills. Because they were practising under conditions that matched the end of a match.
