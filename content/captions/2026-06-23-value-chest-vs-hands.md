HOOK (first line of caption): Two hands or the chest? The answer lives in one cue — and most keepers miss it.

CAPTION:
Every goalkeeper coach has watched it happen: a routine shot, the keeper goes to catch, and the ball squirms through. Not because of nerves. Not because of bad hands. Because the technique chosen didn't match the ball.

Here's the cue that decides everything: **pace and trajectory at the moment of contact.**

If the ball is travelling below waist height with moderate pace — hands behind the ball, fingers spread, W-shape. Clean, controllable. Distribution starts the second you catch it.

If the ball is driven hard at the body — chest. Drop the chin, curve the torso slightly forward, smother it. You're not catching it, you're killing its energy before it can escape.

The mistake coaches see most: keepers defaulting to hands on every ball because it looks more authoritative. It isn't. A chest catch that sticks beats a hand catch that doesn't every time.

**The drill:** In your next shot-stopping block, call the technique before you play the ball — "hands" or "chest" — so keepers are deciding early rather than reacting late. Decision-making is the skill, not just the save.
