HOOK (first line of caption): One hand isn't a last resort — sometimes it's the right call.

CAPTION:
There's a moment every goalkeeper coach recognises. The ball is wide, the dive is full stretch, and there's simply no way both hands get there. That's not a failure of technique — that's physics. The question is whether your keeper is ready to commit to one hand with real purpose.

The problem we see most often isn't the decision to go one-handed. It's the hand arriving soft — fingers passive, wrist loose, palm flinching on contact. A save attempt becomes a spill. A routine block becomes a rebound into danger.

Here's what to drill:

① LEAD WITH THE HEEL OF THE HAND
On wide dives, cue your keeper to drive the heel of the palm through the ball first. Think "push the wall" not "catch the ball." This keeps the wrist firm and redirects the ball wide rather than forward.

② EXTENSION REPS — NO SECOND HAND ALLOWED
Set up a server wide and slightly behind the keeper. Short, sharp serves at hip-to-shoulder height. The keeper must reach — no stepping into it. Repetition trains the shoulder to commit, not hesitate.

③ FINISH THE SAVE, NOT THE DIVE
Film it. You'll often see keepers who make the contact but pull the arm back on landing. The hand needs to stay through the ball into the follow-through. Landing mechanics can undo a technically sound touch.

One-handed saves built on firm contact and correct mechanics become weapons. Built on hope, they stay liabilities.
