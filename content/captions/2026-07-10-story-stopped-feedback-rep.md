HOOK (first line of caption): We used to talk after every single rep. Then we stopped — and the keepers got better.

CAPTION:
For a long time, the session looked like this: keeper makes a save, coach gives feedback, keeper resets, repeat. It felt thorough. It felt like coaching.

But somewhere around the hundredth rep of the season, something became clear. The keepers weren't problem-solving — they were waiting. Waiting for the next instruction before they decided anything for themselves. The feedback loop had become a crutch, and we'd built it for them.

So we pulled back. Longer rest between cues. Whole blocks where the only thing said was the drill start. Keepers had to sit with a poor set position or a slow transfer and figure out what went wrong before anyone told them.

The first few sessions were uncomfortable — for everyone. Keepers looked across after a mistake. Coaches kept their mouths shut. Something had to fill that gap, and eventually it did: the keepers started talking to each other, self-correcting, asking sharper questions when feedback finally came.

That shift — from waiting to thinking — is the thing KEEPIX is built around. The product gives coaches a way to structure reps and track what's actually happening across a session, so when you do speak, it lands on something real. Not a hunch. Not a feeling. Something you both saw.

Less noise. More signal. Better keepers.
