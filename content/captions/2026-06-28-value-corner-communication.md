CAPTION:
When the ball goes to the corner flag, every goalkeeper has roughly four seconds before the delivery. Those four seconds are the difference between a defended set-piece and a goal that "came from nowhere."

Here's the communication order we teach:

1. BACK POST DEFENDER first. One sharp call — "MARK UP" or a name — so they know you've seen the danger side. Back-post runners are the most common source of late goals from corners because no one claimed them early.

2. NEAR POST BLOCKER next. Short, direct: "NEAR POST, STAY." They need to know they're not responsible for coming out — that's your job if the ball dips short.

3. THEN the whole unit. This is your organising call: "ZONAL LEFT" or "MARK UP, HOLD SHAPE" — whatever system you're in. Say it loud, say it once.

The mistake most keepers make is calling everyone at once — a wall of noise that nobody processes. Sequenced communication gives each player a clear instruction at the moment they can act on it.

One drill to build this: walk corners in training with no pressure. Keeper organises out loud, in sequence, every single time. Make it automatic before you make it fast.

If your defenders can predict what you're going to say, you're doing it right.
