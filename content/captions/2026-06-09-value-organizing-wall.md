CAPTION:
Free kicks conceded in dangerous areas are stressful enough. What makes them worse is a wall that looks right but leaves your keeper guessing — unsighted, off their line, or caught mid-shuffle when the ball moves.

Here's how we coach the wall conversation from the keeper's feet up.

**1. The keeper owns the call.**
Before anyone touches the post or starts counting shirts, the keeper sets the number. Two players? Three? That decision belongs to the last line — not the captain, not the nearest midfielder. Train your keepers to call it loud and early, then hold their ground if someone argues.

**2. Count bodies, not jerseys.**
Defenders stack the wall by eye. Attackers exploit the gaps between them. Teach your keeper to confirm the wall is shoulder-to-shoulder before they move to their position — one quick scan, one instruction ("close it up"), then they go. Build that habit in training so it's automatic under pressure.

**3. Position off the post, not the ball.**
A common mistake: keepers set their starting position relative to where the ball is spotted. Instead, anchor off the near post — it gives a consistent reference point regardless of the ball's exact lie. From there, adjust for the angle. Your near post is always your near post.

**4. Rehearse the split second before the kick.**
The wall sets. The keeper is in position. Then the attacking team shifts the ball six inches. What does your keeper do? That reset moment — staying calm, making a micro-adjustment, not reacting too early — is the part that rarely gets trained. Build it into your set-piece reps.

Repetition under realistic pressure is what makes this stick.
