CAPTION:
For a long time, we put distribution at the end of sessions. Last 15 minutes, when everything else was covered. Throw. Kick. Move on.

Then we watched a keeper hesitate — ball in hands, six-yard box, three options available — and roll it straight back to the centre-back under pressure. Safe. Pointless. The chance was gone.

It wasn't a technique problem. The keeper could kick. The keeper could throw. What they couldn't do was read the press quickly enough to choose the right option in time.

We were training the execution. We weren't training the decision.

After that, distribution started every session. Not a long block — three minutes, two options, make a choice. Light pressure first, then more.

By March, the same keeper was switching play with a driven throw inside four seconds. Not because the technique changed. Because the decision came faster.
