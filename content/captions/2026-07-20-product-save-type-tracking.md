HOOK (first line of caption): Your keeper faced 34 crosses last month. How many did your training sessions reflect that?

CAPTION:
Most GK programmes are built on habit — the same rotation of diving saves, distribution reps, and footwork patterns, week after week. That's not a bad thing. But habit without data leaves gaps.

When you can see a breakdown of the saves your keeper actually made across a run of matches — low shots to the near post, deflections, penalty situations, aerial claims — patterns start to emerge. Some situations show up far more than you'd expect. Others you've been drilling heavily barely feature at all.

That's what KEEPIX helps you track. Log the save types coming out of matches, build a picture over weeks and months, and bring that picture into your next session plan. Training doesn't have to be a best guess anymore.

One coach we worked with noticed his keeper was conceding almost exclusively from cutback positions — low, square balls across the six-yard box — yet those rarely appeared in training. Two weeks of targeted reps later, the picture looked different.

The sessions don't change dramatically. The intention behind them does.
