CAPTION:
Close-range shots punish goalkeepers who are still moving when the ball arrives. The fix isn't reflexes. It's footwork — and it starts before the shot is ever struck.

Three principles worth drilling this week:

1 — Short, controlled steps into the set position.
Big strides look athletic. Small, precise steps get your weight balanced over the balls of your feet at the moment the shooter commits. Build it with slow-motion shadow work before you ever add a ball.

2 — Set early, not late.
Keepers want to keep moving "to stay active" — but against a shot from eight yards or less, late movement becomes the problem. The discipline is recognising the shooting moment one beat sooner and planting before the ball leaves the foot.

3 — Hands in front, elbows soft.
In tight spaces there's no time to bring the hands from the hip. Default position: hands forward, palms open, elbows slightly bent. It shortens reaction distance by centimetres — which at six yards is everything.

Run this as a pattern drill: server plays into a striker at the top of the six, keeper adjusts with two or three short steps, sets, and reacts. Repetition builds the muscle memory; decision-making comes later.

What you're looking for is stillness at the right moment. That's the skill.
