HOOK (first line of caption): Most goalkeepers are taught to save the ball. The best ones are taught to save the moment.

CAPTION:
There's a difference between stopping a shot and controlling what happens next. Parrying straight back into the channel — or worse, into the six-yard box — hands the opposition a second chance they haven't earned.

Safe-zone parrying is the habit that changes that. Instead of absorbing the ball, you're steering it. Wide to the post corner. Down and away from the striker's follow-up run. Every deflection has a direction. Your job is to choose it.

Here are three training cues to build the habit:

1. Hand angle before contact. Where your wrist and fingers are angled at the moment of impact decides where the ball goes. Practice the angle without the ball first — it's a conscious skill before it becomes instinct.

2. Identify the safe zone before the shot. In a crossing drill or shooting drill, give your keeper a coloured cone in each corner behind the goal line. Their target isn't "keep it out" — it's "put it there."

3. Train the follow-up, not just the save. After every parry rep, the striker continues their run. The keeper learns quickly that a central rebound is a goal. The feedback is instant, and it sticks.

This is the kind of detail that separates a well-drilled goalkeeper from a reactive one. Small adjustment in technique. Big change in what happens at 87 minutes.
