HOOK (first line of caption): The season I stopped talking so much, my keepers started seeing more.

CAPTION:
It started as an experiment. Not a plan — just a quiet frustration with myself after watching back some session footage. I was filling every rep with words. Set position. Weight forward. Eyes on the ball. Good. Again.

The keepers weren't reading the game. They were waiting for me.

So one pre-season, I made a rule: one cue per rep. Sometimes none. I'd set up the drill, point at the target, and let it run. The first week felt uncomfortable — mostly for me. There were reps I desperately wanted to jump in and fix. I didn't.

By week three something shifted. The keepers started talking to each other between reps. Asking why a ball got away. Adjusting their starting position without being told. The noise in the session came from them, not me.

What helped was having a tool that could hold the repetition steady — consistent service, consistent angles — while I stepped back and just watched. When the technical feedback doesn't have to come from my voice every single time, you start to see which habits are actually landing and which ones were just surviving on your prompts.

Less coaching, more learning. It's not a method. It's just something worth trying.
