HOOK (first line of caption): Your keeper's first step on a near-post cross decides everything that follows.

CAPTION:
Most coaches work on where the keeper ends up — diving, claiming, punching. Few work on where they start. That first movement on a wide delivery is the hinge the whole play turns on.

Here's the positioning framework we come back to every season when working through compact box defense:

1. SET POSITION BEFORE THE CROSS
As the winger shapes to cross, the keeper should be slightly off the line — not sitting against the post, not halfway to the penalty spot. The goal: maximum cover on the near post without surrendering the far-post angle. Toes angled toward the ball. Weight slightly forward.

2. THE FIRST STEP IS LATERAL, NOT FORWARD
Beginners lunge forward. Experienced keepers push sideways — opening the hips to track the ball's flight path while keeping the near post covered. That lateral step buys the split-second read that decides whether to come or hold.

3. STAY BIG, STAY ALIVE
If the keeper commits and the delivery goes short to the penalty spot, they're gone. Staying in a tall, open position keeps both outcomes — claim or recover — genuinely available.

Drill it with your back four in place. Repetition under shape is the only way this becomes instinct under pressure.
