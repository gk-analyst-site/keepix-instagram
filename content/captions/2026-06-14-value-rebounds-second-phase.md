CAPTION:
A keeper makes the save. The ball spills. The striker pounces. Everyone calls it bad luck.

It rarely is.

Reading second-phase situations starts before the shot arrives. Where is the striker's body angle? Is a second attacker running the near post or checking to the penalty spot? The moment you make contact with the ball, your next position is already determined by the answer to those questions — not by reaction alone.

Spilling with intention is a skill that gets under-coached. Pushing wide is the default, but wide into a corridor of pressure is worse than a controlled palm over the bar. Train your keepers to identify the safest quadrant first, then execute. The hands follow the read, not the other way round.

Recovery shape matters as much as the initial save. After a diving stop, the back foot drives back under the hips — you're not crawling back to goal, you're resetting to a live set position. One fluid movement. Work that pattern until it's automatic.

In training, set your second-phase reps up honestly: attacker already moving, ball already spilling, no stopwatch countdown. Messy, realistic, and exactly what match day looks like.

Save the rep. Then save the second one.
