HOOK (first line of caption): The save after the save is a skill. Here's how to train it.

CAPTION:
Every goalkeeper gets beaten. A deflection, a goalmouth scramble, a ball that bounces twice before it even reaches you. The question isn't whether you'll end up on the floor — it's what you do in the next two seconds.

That's the scramble save. And most training sessions never touch it.

Start by normalising going to ground. Build drills where getting beaten on the first shot isn't a failure — it's the starting position. Your keeper dives, the ball is played back in, and the clock starts. Can they reset their base, find the ball, and make a second decision inside two seconds? That's the rep.

Footwork matters here as much as in any other phase. Short, sharp steps back toward post. Hands already moving. Eyes on the ball, not the attacker. The scramble save that looks instinctive is almost always a keeper who trained their recovery mechanics until the body stopped waiting for permission from the brain.

A few things worth drilling this week:
→ Post-save reset: dive, scramble to knees, identify ball, react to second shot.
→ Floor-to-feet: from a seated position, get vertical and set in under 1.5 seconds. Time it.
→ Near-post scramble: keeper dives, assistant plays second ball to the near post, keeper must recover across without standing fully upright.

Second-effort goalkeeping is a mentality — but mentality built on physical habit. Train the habit first.
