HOOK (first line of caption): Two seconds. That's all a keeper has before the press arrives — and the wrong decision kills the build-up before it starts.

CAPTION:
Distribution under press is one of the least-coached moments in the game. We spend hours on shot-stopping, crossing, footwork — and then let goalkeepers figure out the short-versus-long decision on match day.

Here's the framework we come back to every week in training.

**Before the ball arrives — read, not react.**
The goalkeeper's scan should happen before the ball is played back. Who is pressing? Which centre-back has space? Is the 6 showing? That pre-scan turns a reactive panic into a calm decision. If that habit isn't built in training, it won't appear in a 1-0 game with ten minutes left.

**The short ball is the braver choice — when it's right.**
Short to a centre-back under pressure isn't risky because of the distance. It's risky because the keeper hasn't communicated early enough. "Turn" or "man on" before the pass lands changes everything. Practise the communication as hard as you practise the throw.

**The long ball is the reset — not the give-up.**
A driven ball into the channels, played with intent and to a target who knows it's coming, is a weapon. A hopeful clearance is just anxiety. The decision point is simple: if two options are closed and the third is about to be, go long — but go with purpose.

**The two seconds that matter.**
One second before — scan and commit. One second after — move into a new supporting position. That second second is where most keepers are invisible. Don't just watch the outcome. Be available for what comes next.

Train the decision, not just the technique.
