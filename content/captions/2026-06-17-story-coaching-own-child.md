CAPTION:
It was a wet Saturday morning. My own kid was in goal. We were working crossing, and they kept drifting off their line too early — same mistake, third week running.

I gave the correction once. Calm, clear, technical. The way I'd say it to any keeper.
They looked back at me. Not as a keeper looks at a coach. The way a child looks at a parent.

That's the line. And anyone who's been in that position knows how thin it gets.

What we've found — through years of standing on soggy pitches watching goalkeepers grow — is that the best thing you can do for a young keeper, whether they're yours or not, is make the session speak for itself. When the work is structured, when the reps are clean, when they can feel progress in their own body, the feedback lands differently. It's not mum or dad talking. It's the session.

KEEPIX came out of exactly that tension: how do you create an environment where the keeper gets honest, consistent feedback — without every correction becoming personal? The product won't solve the emotion. That's human. But it takes a layer of friction out of the session so the work can do the talking.

If you've coached your own child in goal, you already know. There's no clean answer. You figure it out drill by drill.
