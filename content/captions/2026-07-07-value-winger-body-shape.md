CAPTION:
Before the winger touches the ball, the information is already there. Most keepers are watching the ball. The ones who clean up 1v1s are watching the body — specifically three things that happen in the half-second before the run.

**The planted foot.** When a winger plants wide of the ball, they're loading toward the near post. When the plant is tight and underneath, expect a cut inside. It sounds simple. Train your keeper to read it under pressure and it becomes instinct.

**Hip angle at the moment of receipt.** Open hips — facing the touchline — usually signal an early cross or a run that stays wide. Closed hips, turned slightly inward, are the tell for a drive across the face of goal. Give your keeper a reference point: if you can see both numbers on their shirt, get narrow.

**Shoulder drop.** A low outside shoulder on the approach almost always means pace in behind. A high, level carry means they're going to take a touch and assess. That extra beat is your keeper's invitation to close the angle aggressively.

The work happens in training. Isolate each cue. Run 1v1 reps where your keeper calls the direction out loud — before the winger moves. That verbal commitment builds decision speed better than any reactive drill.

Slow the moment down in the session so it speeds up on Saturday.
