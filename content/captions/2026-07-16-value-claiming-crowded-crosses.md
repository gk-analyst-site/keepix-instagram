CAPTION:
Claiming crosses under pressure is one of the most complete acts in goalkeeping. It asks for timing, physicality, communication, and composure — often in under two seconds. Here's how we break it down in training.

**1. The call — commit early, be loud.**
"KEEPER!" should land before the ball is halfway across. If defenders hear it late, they hesitate and become obstacles. If it never comes, they attack the ball themselves and chaos follows. Train the call in every crossing rep, even when the box is empty. Make it a reflex, not a decision.

**2. Reading the flight, not the kicker.**
Too many keepers watch the foot. Watch the ball from the moment it leaves. That extra fraction of a second tells you arc, pace, and — critically — whether this is yours to take or a ball to hold back on. Honest decision-making beats aggressive decision-making every time.

**3. The jump through traffic.**
Drive off one foot, protect your airspace with the lead knee. You're not jumping to the ball — you're jumping through the crowd to the ball. That knee creates separation without recklessness. Body shape matters as much as timing.

**4. Protect on the way down.**
Most errors happen after the catch. Shoulders forward, ball tucked, chin down. Hit the ground in control, not in hope. If contact comes, you've already won the first battle — don't lose the second.

Reps in a clean box build technique. Reps in a crowded box build keepers.
