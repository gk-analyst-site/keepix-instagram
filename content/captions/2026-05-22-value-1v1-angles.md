HOOK (first line of caption): Most keepers lose 1v1s before the striker even touches the ball.
CAPTION:
It's not reaction speed. It's not dive technique. It's starting position — and it's the most overlooked detail in goalkeeper coaching.

Here's what we see constantly: a striker breaks through, and the keeper backpedals toward goal instead of holding their ground and narrowing the angle. By the time they set, the striker has all four corners to aim at. The save becomes a lottery.

The fix is simpler than most coaches think. As the striker approaches, the keeper's job is to shrink the target — not protect it. Moving off the line toward the ball, staying on a central angle bisector, and arriving in a composed set position before the striker decides removes options. A well-angled keeper makes the goal look small. A flat-footed keeper standing on the line makes it look enormous.

Drill it this way: put a cone at the centre of the goal line and a cone on each post. Run string from each post cone to a ball placed 10m out. The angle bisector is the line between the two strings. That's your keeper's optimal position for that ball location. Repeat for different release points. Do it enough and the geometry becomes instinct.

The adjustment takes one session to understand and a whole career to perfect. Start this week.
