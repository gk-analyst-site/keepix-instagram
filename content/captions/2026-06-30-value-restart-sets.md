HOOK (first line of caption): Most teams concede their first goal within four minutes of kick-off. Here's why restart sets are a goalkeeper training priority — not an afterthought.

CAPTION:
The whistle blows, the ball rolls forward, and your back line resets. Looks straightforward. But kick-off is one of the highest-risk moments of any half — and goalkeepers are often the last line of a plan nobody actually practised.

The problem isn't athleticism. It's positioning and communication. At kick-off, your defensive shape is compressed and your centre-backs are rarely in their natural rhythm. A quick combination, one runner in behind, and your keeper is suddenly in a 1v1 they didn't see coming.

Three things to work on in your next session:

① Set position before the whistle. Where is your keeper standing when the ref signals play? Train them to take two deliberate steps off their line — not reactive, but proactive — before the ball moves.

② Define the trigger. Agree on one clear cue (the second pass, the first switch of play) that tells your keeper to hold shape vs. step up. Ambiguity kills decision-making.

③ Communication window. In the first 90 seconds of each half, verbal output from your keeper should be constant. Ball near, step, tuck — whatever your language is. Drill the talking, not just the footwork.

Restart sets don't need extra time on the schedule. Five minutes at the top of any session, with your outfield players in kick-off positions, builds the habit quickly.
