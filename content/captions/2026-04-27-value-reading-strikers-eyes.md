CAPTION:
By the time the ball leaves the boot, the decision is already made. The good keepers aren't faster — they start moving earlier, on cues the striker gives away half a second before contact.

Three things we teach keepers to watch, in this order:

1. The eyes — right before striking, most strikers glance at the corner they're aiming for. Not a stare. A flicker. Train keepers to catch that flicker in peripheral vision without locking onto the face.

2. The shoulder — the front shoulder rotates toward the target. A closed shoulder usually means near post. An opened one usually means far. It's not a law, but it's a tell more often than coaches admit.

3. The standing foot — planted ahead of the ball means low and driven. Planted level with it means lifted and placed. This one's the most reliable cue and the one keepers learn last.

Add one cue per week. Don't overload. Read in layers.
