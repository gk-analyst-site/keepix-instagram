CAPTION:
Wet-weather handling separates keepers who train for it from keepers who hope for it. Here's what we coach when the rain comes down.

**Grip and contact point.**
On a dry ball you can afford a clean palm-catch. On a wet ball, trust fingertip contact first — spread wide, thumbs angled behind the ball, let the fingers soak up pace before the palms close. Pressing the ball into the chest too early on a heavy delivery is where drops happen. Patience on contact wins.

**Know when to parry — and commit to it.**
If the ball is slick, hit hard, and arriving above shoulder height, don't fight physics. A clean two-handed parry around the post beats a fumbled "catch" into the six-yard box every time. Drill it. Make parrying to a safe angle a decision your keeper owns — not a last resort they apologise for.

**Footwork under the ball.**
Wet grass reduces traction, so footwork patterns need to be shorter and quicker. Get your keeper working small, rapid adjustment steps rather than long strides as the ball travels. The set position has to be reached a half-second earlier than usual — because recovery if your weight is wrong is twice as costly on a slick surface.

**One session idea.**
Mix service types: low skimmers, looping crosses, driven cut-backs. Hose down your practice ball or just train on a wet morning and let the conditions do the work. Watch where contact is breaking down — grip, footwork, or decision-making — then isolate that piece.

Rain sessions aren't inconvenient. They're the sessions that matter.
