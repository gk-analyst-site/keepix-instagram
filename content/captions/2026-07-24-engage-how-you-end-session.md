HOOK (first line of caption): How do you end a goalkeeper session — and does it actually matter?

CAPTION:
The drill is done. The cones are down. Your keeper is breathing hard and you've got about 90 seconds before they disappear to join the outfield.

What you do in those 90 seconds is something we think about a lot.

Some coaches finish on a game — something competitive, something the keeper controls, so they leave on a high regardless of how the session went. Others end with a specific challenge: beat your own record, one clean catch from the last ball. Some don't say anything tactical at all. They just ask how it felt.

There's no single right answer. But we've noticed that keepers who finish with clarity — even one clear thought to carry into the week — tend to come back sharper.

So we're asking you: how do you close a session? A game, a challenge, a quiet word at the gate? And why that way?

Drop it in the comments. We read every one.
