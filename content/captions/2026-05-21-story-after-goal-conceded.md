CAPTION:
The ball is in the net. Your keeper is standing at the six-yard box, staring at the ground or already walking back with eyes that give nothing away — which somehow feels worse.

What do you do with the next forty-five seconds?

Most coaches default to one of two things: silence because they don't want to make it bigger, or a quick tactical fix — "stay narrow, they got in behind you." Both are understandable. Neither is quite enough.

What we've learned, watching keepers over years of sessions and matchdays, is that the forty-five seconds after conceding is rarely about the goal. It's about the next save. The goalkeeper's brain is either resetting or spiralling, and which one happens often depends on what the person behind the dugout does right now.

A short walk toward them — not a sprint, not a shout across the pitch — already signals: you're not in trouble, I'm still here. Eye contact. One sentence maximum. Something that pulls their attention forward, not backward. "Next action. Let's go." Not dismissal of the mistake. Just a redirect.

The technical debrief comes at half-time. What you're protecting right now is their presence in the game — because a keeper who checks out mentally after a goal will cost you more than the one already on the scoreboard.

The work starts on the training pitch. But the forty-five seconds? That's yours.
