HOOK (first line of caption): The worst sessions teach the most.

CAPTION:
Wet pitch. Cold. Half the session plan didn't work because the surface was too slow for what we'd built around footwork patterns.

We improvised. Scrapped the footwork block, moved to handling under pressure, added a crossing sequence we hadn't planned. The keeper had to adapt. So did we.

What we noticed: the keeper was sharper in the second half of that session than in any session that week. Not despite the chaos — because of it.

When the plan falls apart, the keeper has to read the session as it actually is, not as they expected it to be. That's match-day thinking. That's exactly what we want.

The sessions we control too tightly sometimes train the plan, not the keeper.

We haven't over-planned since.
