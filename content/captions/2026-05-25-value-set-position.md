HOOK (first line of caption): Most keepers are set. But set for what — a save, or a stumble?

CAPTION:
The set position gets taught early and questioned rarely. That's the problem. What most coaches accept is a stance that looks composed but leaves the keeper a half-beat behind the moment the ball leaves the striker's foot.

Here's what we keep coming back to after years on the training pitch.

WEIGHT DISTRIBUTION
Heels down is passive. The keeper looks ready but the ankles are locked, the hips are back, and any explosive first step costs an extra fraction of a second. Weight should sit just ahead of the midfoot — not a lean, a loading. Think coiled, not crouched.

HAND HEIGHT
Hands below the waist is a coaching habit worth questioning. By the time a driven shot arrives, low hands have to travel further and they travel across the body, not through it. Hands level with the lower ribs, soft elbows slightly forward, means the hands are already in the corridor where most shots land. They react, they don't chase.

THE MOMENT OF LOADING
The set isn't a position you hold — it's a moment you hit. Timing the final weight shift to land as the striker's plant foot hits the ground is the difference between a keeper who reads the shot and one who responds to it. Use your sessions to train the trigger, not just the shape.

Work these three points separately before you combine them. One rep of a correct loading moment is worth twenty reps of a tidy-looking static stance.
