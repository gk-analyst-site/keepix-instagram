CAPTION:
Most goalkeeper programmes run on instinct — good instinct, built over years on the pitch. But when you sit down to plan a full four-week block, instinct alone leaves gaps. That's where KEEPIX comes in.

Week 1 is your baseline technical block. Short, sharp sessions around footwork and set position. You're not loading the keeper here — you're building the movement vocabulary they'll need when the work gets heavier.

Week 2 introduces overload. More reps, tighter angles, faster service. This is where KEEPIX earns its place: you can log exactly what you're loading, adjust on the fly, and make sure the difficulty is intentional — not just random hard work.

Week 3 is your peak week. Everything is dialled up. Crossing pressure, 1v1 decisions under fatigue, distribution under time constraints. The kind of session that tells you — and your keeper — where they actually stand.

Week 4 pulls back. Recovery focus. Lower volume, higher quality. Confidence-building reps. A chance to review what KEEPIX has tracked across the block and have a proper conversation with your keeper about what improved and what the next four weeks should target.

Structure doesn't limit you as a coach. It frees you to coach.
