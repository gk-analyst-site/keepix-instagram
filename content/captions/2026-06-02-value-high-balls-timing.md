CAPTION:
Dealing with crosses is one of those moments where everything happens at once: the flight of the ball, bodies pressing in, the ground under your feet. When it breaks down, it usually breaks down early.

Here are three things worth drilling this week.

**1. Read the flight, commit to the line.**
Hesitation is the real problem. A keeper who takes a decisive angle — even a slightly imperfect one — is harder to challenge than one who drifts. In training, call your line out loud. It builds the habit and tells your back four you're coming.

**2. The standing leg decides everything.**
Plant it too close and your jump is cramped. Too far and you're off-balance before you've left the ground. Work on a consistent last stride — it's the same principle as a triple jumper's penultimate step. Slow it down in training until the position is automatic.

**3. Protect the catch with your body, not just your hands.**
High-traffic situations mean contact. Knee drive shields the space beneath the ball and stops your arms being stripped on the way up. Catch, tuck, land — the sequence matters as much as the jump itself.

The environment you train in has to test all three together. Static repetition builds the individual skill; contested repetition builds the goalkeeper.
