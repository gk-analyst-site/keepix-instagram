CAPTION:
We see it at every pre-season: keepers standing in a line, doing the same shuttle runs and passing drills as the strikers. Speed, touch, personality under pressure — none of it gets a fair look. By the end, you're guessing.

KEEPIX was built around a different idea. Tryout footage is only useful if it's structured — same angles, same drill sequence, same moment of contact captured every time. When the conditions are consistent, the comparisons are honest.

Here's the order we recommend for a GK-specific protocol:

First, set position and footwork — see how they move before the ball arrives. Then shot-stopping in a controlled corridor: ground, mid-height, top corners. Then distribution — both hands and feet, under no pressure. Finally, decision-making under fatigue, when form starts to break.

What to ignore in the first session: communication volume, size, how they carry themselves walking in. Those tell you almost nothing at tryout stage. Watch the feet. Watch the hands. Watch what happens after a mistake.

With KEEPIX, every session gets logged the same way. So when you're comparing two keepers from different days, you're comparing like for like — not memory versus memory.
