HOOK (first line of caption): Most crossing sessions stop at "get to the ball." KEEPIX takes it three phases further.

CAPTION:
Crossing is one of the most mismanaged areas of goalkeeper training. Coaches know the rep volume. The movement patterns. The calls. But structuring a session that actually builds decision-making — under real aerial pressure, in sequence — is harder than it looks.

Here's how we structure a crossing block with KEEPIX.

**Phase 1 — Positioning anchor.**
Before the ball is even in the air, the keeper establishes a reference point relative to the back post. KEEPIX gives coaches a fixed, repeatable delivery angle so the keeper can train that anchor position with consistency. No guessing. Same flight path, same starting problem.

**Phase 2 — Flight-read and attack.**
Once the anchor is set, the rep shifts to reading the cross early and committing to a line. This is where most training breaks down — keepers react to where the ball lands rather than attacking where it's going. Consistent delivery from KEEPIX lets coaches isolate this moment and coach it cleanly.

**Phase 3 — Decision under aerial pressure.**
Now you add bodies. The keeper has the positioning habit and the flight-read habit. KEEPIX maintains its delivery while outfield players create contact pressure. The keeper has to hold their line, time the jump, and make the call — catch, punch, or leave — under real conditions.

Three phases. One session. Each one building on the last.

That's the structure we set out to make repeatable for coaches at every level.
