Handling is the one area that falls through the cracks when time is tight. Here are three drills you can run back-to-back with minimal setup — and genuine transfer to match day.

─────────────────────
1. W-CATCH (5 min)
─────────────────────
Keeper in their set position. Serve varied deliveries to chest and shoulder height, alternating hands on the server side. Focus cue: thumbs together behind the ball, elbows in, pull through the midline. Ten clean catches, then swap height. This isn't about volume — it's about resetting good hand shape under mild time pressure.

─────────────────────
2. PARRY-TO-RECOVERY (5 min)
─────────────────────
Serve a firm shot to the strong hand, keeper parries wide intentionally, then recovers set position before the next ball arrives (3-second window). Progress by shrinking the window to 2 seconds. The goal isn't the parry itself — it's the footwork and composure that follow it. That's where clean second-save opportunities are won or lost.

─────────────────────
3. LOW-SCOOP UNDER FATIGUE (5 min)
─────────────────────
After a brief burst — five lateral shuffles or a short sprint — the keeper must scoop a low, rolling ball cleanly. Hands behind the ball, little finger to little finger, body committed low early. Running this at the end of a 15-minute block ensures you're training the skill in the physical state it actually appears in a game: legs working, lungs working.

Three drills. One quality block. That's all you need.
