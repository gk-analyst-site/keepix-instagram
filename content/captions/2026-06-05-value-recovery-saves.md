CAPTION:
Most goals from rebounds aren't just bad luck — they're the result of a keeper who hasn't practiced what comes after the dive. Recovery saves are a skill, and like every other skill, they're trainable.

Here are three things we drill with KEEPIX keepers to sharpen that second-effort save:

1. CONTROLLED LANDING → DRIVE UP
Teach your keeper to land on the outside of the thigh, not the hip bone. One fluid roll, then two hands to the ground, then drive up through the outside leg. Sloppy landings mean slow recovery — the ground is your launching pad, not your resting place.

2. EYES UP BEFORE YOU'RE UP
Keepers often find their feet before they find the ball. Reverse that habit. Track the ball from the floor. Know where the striker is before your hips are even off the turf. Two seconds of awareness saves more goals than any amount of athleticism.

3. SHORT RESET, NOT FULL SET POSITION
You don't have time to reset into a textbook stance. Practice a compact, low base — weight forward, hands wide and ready — as a dedicated "recovery set." It's a different shape to your normal starting position, and it needs its own repetitions.

Run these as a sequence: dive to either side, controlled landing, recovery set, immediate low shot from 4 yards. Ten reps each side. Watch how many half-chances suddenly stop going in.
