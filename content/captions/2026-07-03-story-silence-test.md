HOOK (first line of caption): There's a moment every goalkeeper goes through — and most coaches get wrong.

CAPTION:
A wet Tuesday in February. Your keeper has let in soft goals three sessions in a row. You've watched the clips. You've drawn the arrows on the whiteboard. You've had the conversation.

Nothing sticks.

Most coaches — us included, early on — reach for more words. More explanation. More encouragement, more correction, more time on the video. The assumption is that if something isn't changing, you haven't said the right thing yet.

Then one session, almost by accident, we stopped talking. Set the reps up. Pointed at the cone. Let them work.

The silence wasn't cold. It wasn't punishment. It was just: here's the task, here's the ball, let's see what your body already knows.

Something shifted. Not because we'd finally found the perfect cue — but because we'd stopped interrupting the process with our own anxiety about it.

Bad streaks in goalkeepers are rarely technical. The hands are fine. The feet remember. What's broken is the conversation happening inside their head — and more words from the outside just add to the noise.

The silence test: run a full sequence. Say nothing unless it's genuinely dangerous. Watch what they fix on their own. You'll be surprised what was already in there, waiting.

Some things can't be coached out of a player. They have to be worked out. Your job in those moments isn't to solve it — it's to build the environment where they can.
