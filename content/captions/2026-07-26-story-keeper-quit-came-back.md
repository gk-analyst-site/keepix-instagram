CAPTION:
It was the end of October. Training had felt flat for months — the kind of flat where you go through every rep correctly and feel nothing. So he stopped. Packed the gloves away. Told the coach he needed time.

For a full season he watched from the stands, refereed Sunday league, coached a group of under-9s who had no idea what a set position was. He wasn't thinking about his game. He was just living around football without the pressure of performing in it.

When he came back the following pre-season, the first thing anyone noticed wasn't his hands or his footwork. It was his eyes. He was watching the striker's hips differently. He was reading the shape of the attack earlier. The break hadn't made him rusty — it had made him quieter in the right places.

He said something after his first session back that we've thought about since: "I stopped trying to fix myself and started remembering why I liked being in goal."

That's the thing nobody tells you about development. Progress isn't always a straight line of reps and data. Sometimes it's a season watching from the outside, remembering what drew you to the position in the first place.

Whatever stage you're at — keeper, coach, or both — it's worth asking: are you training from love or from anxiety right now? The answer shapes everything.
