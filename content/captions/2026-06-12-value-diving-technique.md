CAPTION:
A low shot trickling inside the post and a driven effort into the top corner both demand a dive — but treating them the same way is one of the most common technical gaps we see in developing keepers.

Here's the distinction we coach:

**The Collapse Save (low shots)**
Stay on your feet as long as possible. When you go, fold the lead leg underneath and let your body lower to the ground — don't launch. Your saving hand leads palm-out, gloves down behind the ball, second hand wraps over the top. The closer the ball, the later you go. Power is wasted energy here. Efficiency wins.

**The Power Dive (top corner)**
This is the one that looks good on a poster — but it has to be earned. Strong push off the outside foot, hip opens, you're generating height and distance at the same time. Hand position: catching frame first, fingers spread, thumb and index forming your window. If it's unreachable, punch clean. Commit fully or you'll half-dive and watch it go in anyway.

**The mistake coaches see most:**
Keepers collapsing on top-corner attempts (losing reach) and launching on low shots (getting beaten at the near post). Drill the decision, not just the movement.

One session tip: put cones at shin height and chest height either side of the keeper. Feed low balls in — they stay below the low cone. Feed driven shots high — they must clear the high cone on the way out. Simple visual cue, instant feedback.
