CAPTION:
Catching versus parrying is one of the most debated topics on the training pitch. Coaches have strong opinions. So do keepers. But the truth is, it's not a preference — it's a read.

Here's the framework we coach:

**Catch when you can control the outcome.**
Ball speed moderate, trajectory predictable, body behind the line. If you can get two hands on it and your feet are set, take it. Possession won. Counter starts immediately. The simplest correct decision is still the best decision.

**Parry when catching introduces more risk than deflecting.**
Pace, spin, awkward height, unstable footing — any of these shift the calculation. Now the priority is *direction* of deflection. Wide of the post if the angle allows. Over the bar only when wide is genuinely off the table. A parry to the centre of the box is not a save — it's a second chance gifted.

**The read that separates good keepers from great ones?**
They're making this call during the build-up to the shot — reading the shooter's hip angle, the service speed, the position of their own feet — not reacting after contact. Training should put keepers in situations where they *rehearse the decision*, not just the technique.

In your next session: set up shots from the same position at three different speeds. No cue from you on what to do. Let them read, decide, and debrief together after. The conversation that follows will tell you everything about where their decision-making is right now.
