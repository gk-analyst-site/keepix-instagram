CAPTION:
Every goalkeeper coach knows the feeling. You run a solid set of drills, the keeper executes them well — and somewhere in week four or five, the effort is still there but the edge is gone. Not laziness. Just familiarity. The body learns to coast when it knows what's coming next.

That's the problem KEEPIX was built around.

Inside a session, KEEPIX organises work across four drill categories — footwork, shot-stopping, crossing, and distribution — so no single area dominates a block. When you plan a week, you can see at a glance whether you've been leaning too hard on one column. That visual check alone changes how coaches structure the month.

Difficulty progression is built into the session flow rather than left to memory. As a keeper grows more comfortable at a given level, the next step is already queued. You're not guessing when to raise the bar — the structure tells you. And crucially, it tells you when *not* to: there's a reset function for sessions after a tough match or a difficult week, so you can step back without losing the thread of where you were.

The result isn't a more complicated session. It's a session that stays honest — one where the keeper has to concentrate, not just perform a routine they've already memorised.
