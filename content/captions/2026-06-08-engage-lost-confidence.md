A run of bad results. A few goals that hit the net in ugly ways. You can see it in training the day after — the hesitation on crosses, the slightly wider stance on set pieces, the eyes that stop trusting the ball.

Confidence in a goalkeeper isn't loud when it's there. But you notice immediately when it's gone.

We've seen coaches handle this a dozen different ways — reducing the load, going back to basics, changing the environment entirely. Some swear by high-rep shot-stopping to rebuild rhythm. Others pull the keeper out of the spotlight for a session and focus purely on footwork and distribution with zero pressure.

There's no single answer. And that's exactly why we're asking.
