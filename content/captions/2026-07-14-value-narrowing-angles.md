CAPTION:
1v1s are won before the save. They're won in the approach — the line you take, the pace you use, and the exact moment you stop moving.

Here are three things we coach every week with our keepers:

1 — Arc, don't sprint straight.
A straight-line rush hands the attacker the angle. Come off your line on a slight curve toward the near post. This closes the far corner as you travel, so by the time you're in range you've already taken half the goal away. It feels slower. It isn't.

2 — Know your decision point.
There's a window — roughly 8 to 10 yards out from the ball — where you shift from approaching to holding. Beyond it, the attacker can lift over you. Inside it, you can read the touch and react. Arrive there under control, weight slightly forward, hands low and wide.

3 — The hold is active, not passive.
"Hold your position" doesn't mean stop thinking. When you set, you're reading the attacker's hips, their next touch, their eyes. You're ready to drop if they chip or go early if they put their head down. Still feet, live mind.

The approach is coachable. The timing is trainable. Put your keepers in these reps deliberately and the panic starts to disappear.
