CAPTION:
A U10 keeper and a U16 keeper are not the same athlete — and they shouldn't be coached the same way.

With younger keepers, the priority is movement confidence and joy. Short bursts. Big reactions. Lots of repetition with low consequence. At U10, you're building the habit of getting behind the ball and staying tall — that's it. Everything else is noise.

By U16, the work shifts. Now you're loading decision-making. Distribution under pressure. Reading the striker's body shape in a 1v1. The physical capacity is there — the challenge is building composure on top of it.

KEEPIX lets you structure both sessions from the same unit. For the younger group, we keep the intensity low and the variety high — short intervals, clear visual cues, movement patterns that feel like a game. For the older group, we extend the work periods, increase the demand on footwork and set position, and build in moments where the keeper has to choose — not just react.

The danger for most coaches is defaulting to one session style and scaling the numbers up or down. Age-appropriate coaching isn't about making it easier or harder. It's about targeting what each keeper actually needs in that window of development.

Plan the session with the player in mind first. The reps will follow.
