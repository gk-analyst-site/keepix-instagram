CAPTION:
A clean shot you can read. A deflection you have to react to.
That split-second change of direction is where a lot of goalkeepers lose the save — not because of bad hands, but because the body was already committed.

Here are three things we work on at KEEPIX to handle deflections better:

1. STAY TALL, LATE.
The instinct is to go early — to dive before you know where it's going. On deflections, the goalkeeper who holds their height longest wins. Keep your chest up, weight slightly forward, and resist the urge to drop until the ball gives you information.

2. SHORT ADJUSTING STEPS.
When the trajectory changes, big strides kill you. Teach your keepers to reset with two or three short, sharp steps — not a lunge. It keeps the hips under control and gives the hands a real chance.

3. TRUST THE HANDS, NOT THE BODY.
A lot of goalkeepers try to save with their chest or block with their whole frame. On late deflections, the hands are faster. Keep them open, in front of the body, and let them do the work. A relaxed wrist handles redirection far better than a stiff arm.

These aren't complex. But they take deliberate repetition — which is exactly what we design KEEPIX sessions around.
