HOOK (first line of caption): Most periodization models are built for outfield players. KEEPIX helps you build one that actually fits a goalkeeper.

CAPTION:
A striker's pre-season and a goalkeeper's pre-season are not the same thing. Yet most clubs run them identically. Reaction work, footwork volume, crossing exposure — all of it lands on the keeper at the same intensity, at the same time, as the midfielders doing fitness laps. That's a gap KEEPIX was designed to close.

Here's the framework we work from across a full season:

BASE PHASE — build the physical and technical platform. High footwork volume, set-position work, slow-tempo crossing. The keeper is learning shape and loading the body. KEEPIX lets you log which movement patterns you're hitting and spot technical drift early before it becomes habit.

LOAD PHASE — volume peaks, complexity rises. 1v1 pressure, distribution under fatigue, crossing in tight windows. This is where keepers either make the jump or start compensating. With KEEPIX, you can see whether the work is landing — or whether you're just accumulating minutes without progress.

PEAK PHASE — sharpen, not add. Volume drops, sharpness stays. Sessions get shorter and more specific. The data from earlier phases tells you which two or three things actually need your attention in the final weeks before the competitive run.

TAPER — trust the work. Reduce contact, maintain rhythm. KEEPIX helps you resist the temptation to cram. If the foundation is there, the numbers will tell you.

Periodization isn't just for fitness coaches. Keepers deserve a season plan built around what they actually do.
