HOOK (first line of caption): Your keeper is already at a disadvantage before the ball is placed on the spot. Here's how to close that gap.

CAPTION:
The penalty is the only moment in football where the opponent has near-total control of timing, direction, and disguise. Your keeper knows this. The striker knows this. The mathematics aren't kind.

So what do you actually coach?

You coach the routine. Not the save — the routine. Because what a keeper can control in those 8–12 seconds before the whistle blows is the quality of their own mental state. That starts with the walk to the line. Slow. Deliberate. Hands relaxed. Eyes on the ball, not the striker's face.

Then the wait. This is where most keepers lose the battle invisibly. The referee hasn't blown. The crowd is loud. The striker is doing something — bouncing the ball, stepping back, adjusting their run-up. Train your keeper to use that window, not endure it. Two deep breaths. Weight slightly forward. A cue word they've chosen themselves, rehearsed in training, not invented under pressure.

What you genuinely cannot control: which corner the striker picks. What you can control: the dive decision trigger, the pre-set lean, the reset after a miss by either side.

In your next session, build the routine deliberately. Set the scenario. Blow the whistle late. Let them stand there. Let the discomfort arrive — and practice moving through it, not away from it. The save rate may not double. But the keeper who walks off after conceding a penalty looking composed? That's a keeper who doesn't carry it into the next 90 minutes.

That's worth coaching.
