CAPTION:
Most keepers have one footwork pattern they're comfortable with. It works for 70% of situations — and costs them the other 30%.

The three patterns we drill separately before combining:

1. The shuffle & set — lateral movement across a short distance (1-3m), finishing in set position before the ball arrives. Used for angle adjustment as the play develops. Most keepers do this well when fresh; few do it well under fatigue.

2. The cross-step — one leg crosses the other for wider lateral coverage (3-6m). Faster than a shuffle across that distance, but requires the keeper to recover set position afterwards. The recovery is what we actually drill most.

3. The drop-step — used when the ball is moving behind the keeper (high balls, back-post crosses). First step is backwards, not sideways. Keepers who haven't drilled this instinctively step sideways and lose the ball into the sun.

Each has a trigger. Each has a distance range it suits best. Training all three gives keepers options — not just a default.
