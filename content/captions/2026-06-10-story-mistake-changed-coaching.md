HOOK (first line of caption): The day I realised I was coaching the save — not the keeper.

CAPTION:
It was a wet Tuesday evening, under floodlights that flickered every time the wind picked up. Our keeper had just spilled a routine shot for the third time in the session. Same mistake. Same correction from me. Same result.

I ran through the checklist again — hand position, elbow angle, body weight. Textbook stuff. And the keeper nodded, reset, and spilled it a fourth time.

That was the moment I stopped talking.

I walked over, stood next to them, and asked one question: "What are you seeing when the ball leaves the striker's foot?" The answer floored me. They weren't watching the ball at all — they were watching me, waiting for the correction before it even came.

I'd spent twenty minutes coaching a technique. I hadn't spent twenty seconds coaching the person in front of me.

From that evening on, the session changed. Less instruction before the rep. More honest conversation after it. Questions instead of answers. Room for the keeper to feel the problem rather than just hear the fix.

The saves got cleaner. More importantly, the keeper started owning them.

That shift — from fixing to understanding — is built into everything we do at KEEPIX. The tool creates the repetitions. The coach creates the connection.
