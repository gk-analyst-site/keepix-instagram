CAPTION:
Most cross errors aren't mistakes of athleticism. They're mistakes of timing — and timing starts with information gathering before the delivery, not after it.

Here are the three decision points every keeper needs to identify early:

1 — THE DELIVERY SHAPE
Watch the server's body angle, plant foot, and hip line as they approach. Is the cross driven flat, looped, or cut back? A keeper who reads the delivery shape early buys an extra half-second. That half-second is the difference between claiming and watching.

2 — THE FLIGHT ZONE
The ball's first metre of flight tells you almost everything. Will it reach the six? Die at the edge of the area? Move away from you late? Don't wait for the ball to arrive — project where it's going and start moving to that point. Stand still and you've already lost.

3 — THE BODY COUNT
How many shirts between you and the ball? Where is your nearest defender? A clean run means go. Bodies in traffic means hold your line, attack the second ball, and dominate your area vocally. The keeper who knows their own box controls their box.

Train these decision points in isolation first — deliberately slow the rep so keepers can narrate what they see. Then speed it up. Then put defenders and attackers in. The decision framework doesn't change; the time to execute it gets compressed.

That's the work.
