CAPTION:
Set position for long-range shots is one of the most under-coached details in the game. Coaches spend hours on footwork and distribution — then wonder why their keeper gets beaten low to the near post from 25 yards.

Here's what we look at in every session.

**Depth off the line.**
Too close and you shrink the angles in your own head. Too deep and the top corners open up. For most keepers, 1–2 steps off the line gives you the best coverage without selling short or high. Find your keeper's natural set point — then make it a habit, not a guess.

**Weight distribution.**
Feet shoulder-width apart, slight bend in the knees, weight forward through the balls of the feet. Not bouncing — loaded. Think of a sprinter in the blocks: still, but ready. If your keeper's weight is back on their heels when the shot comes, they're already reacting late.

**Staying big when the shot arrives early.**
Long-range efforts often come from a half-cleared set piece or a quick switch. Your keeper may not have time to reset. Train them to hold shape through the chaos — arms out slightly, eyes through the ball, not at the shooter. The instinct to flinch or collapse early is what creates saves that look harder than they are.

Work these three details together in training. A few deliberate reps each session is enough — the goal is to make the set position feel like the most natural place your keeper can be.
