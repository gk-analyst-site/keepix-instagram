CAPTION:
Every goalkeeper coach has seen it — a clean stop, textbook technique, and then the rebound trickles in because the keeper was still admiring the first save. Reading the second ball is a skill, and like every skill, it can be trained.

Here's the framework we use at KEEPIX.

📍 SCAN ON CONTACT
The moment your hands meet the ball, your eyes should already be moving — not following the ball back into your chest, but scanning: Where's the striker? Where's the shape? Where's the pressure coming from? This is the habit to build in every rep.

📍 PARRY WITH PURPOSE
If you know you can't hold it, direct the ball toward dead space — wide of the post, away from the corridor of danger. An uncontrolled parry is an invitation. A coached parry is a clearance.

📍 THE RESET WINDOW
You have roughly one second between the first save and the rebound arriving. Use it to drop your hips, reload your feet, and arrive in your set position *before* the shot — not as it's hit. This is the window goalkeepers skip in training because it's uncomfortable to drill. Don't skip it.

Try this in your next session: after every rep, the keeper must touch both posts before the next ball comes in. It forces them to move, reset, and re-engage — not stand and spectate.

The best shot-stoppers don't make one save. They make the save that matters most.
