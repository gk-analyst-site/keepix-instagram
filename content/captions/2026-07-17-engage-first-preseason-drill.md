CAPTION:
That first session sets the tone for everything — the standard you expect, the habits you're building, the culture in your training group.

Some coaches go straight to footwork. Get the feet moving, remind the body what it feels like to be sharp and switched on. Others start with handling — simple catches, two hands, eyes focused, reconnecting with the ball after a long off-season.

A few coaches we've spoken to swear by a passing and distribution circuit first. Get the keepers talking, moving, involved. Make them feel like footballers from minute one.

And then there are coaches who lead with a long, unhurried warm-up — joint mobility, lateral shuffles, light footwork patterns — before a ball is even introduced. Build the engine before you test it.

There's no single right answer. But there are a hundred interesting ones.
