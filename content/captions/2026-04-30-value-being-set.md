HOOK (first line of caption): "Be set" is the most misunderstood cue in keeper coaching.

CAPTION:
It doesn't mean stop moving. A keeper who stops is a keeper who's late.

Being set is about being ready to move in any direction — feet alive under you, weight where you can leave it, hands where they can work. It's a dynamic shape, not a static one.

Three things we check every time:

1. Balance — weight on the balls of the feet, not the heels. If a coach could push the keeper backwards with one finger, they're not set.

2. Stance width — roughly shoulder-width, slightly wider for taller keepers. Too narrow and lateral movement collapses. Too wide and first-step power disappears.

3. Centre of mass — hips low enough that the keeper can explode sideways without rising first. If the first move is up instead of across, the set is too tall.

Teach the feeling, not just the picture. Ask the keeper where their weight is before the ball is struck. The answer tells you everything.
