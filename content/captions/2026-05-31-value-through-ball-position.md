HOOK (first line of caption): Your keeper's starting position decides the game before the ball does.

CAPTION:
When a through ball splits the defence, most coaches focus on the keeper's decision to come or stay. But the decision was already made — the moment the striker received possession 30 yards out.

Starting position is where you win or lose those moments.

Hold the line too deep and your keeper is mopping up behind a leaky back four, reacting rather than acting. Hold it too high and a lobbed ball over the top becomes a sprint they can't win. The answer isn't a fixed distance — it's reading the game.

Three cues we coach at KEEPIX:

1. Ball position. When the ball is in front of the last line, keeper edges up to compress the space behind the defence. When it drops to feet in behind, keeper reads the angle and adjusts — immediately.

2. Striker's body shape. A striker's first touch opening away from goal is your keeper's cue to hold or step up. A touch that points toward the channel? Drop a yard. Now.

3. The space between keeper and last defender. Keep it honest — roughly 5 to 8 yards in most open-play situations. Too tight and a flicked header sends you scrambling. Too wide and there's a pocket of ground no one owns.

The best keepers aren't brave — they're positioned. Brave is a band-aid for poor starting positions.

Film one session this week. Watch where your keeper stands before the through ball arrives. That's where the work is.
