CAPTION:
Most back-pass errors aren't technical — they're decisions made too late. By the time the ball arrives, the keeper should already know where it's going next. Here's what we drill with KEEPIX.

**1. Read the picture before the ball leaves the defender's foot.**
As the back-pass is played, the keeper scans: press from the front? Winger tucking in? Full-back overlapping? That picture dictates everything. The decision window is closing the moment the ball moves.

**2. Body shape on approach — open yourself to the pitch.**
Angle your run to the ball so your hips stay open toward the play, not square to your own goal. A closed body shape kills your distribution options and telegraphs a long clearance under no real pressure.

**3. First touch is direction, not just control.**
The touch should shift the ball onto the preferred foot and into space — simultaneously. A static first touch that stops the ball dead invites pressure. One clean touch, already pointed at your target.

**4. The "last look" cue.**
We coach keepers to take a final scan at the moment of touch — not after. If you're still searching for options while the ball is under your foot, it's too late. Build the habit in training so it's automatic under match pressure.

Drill this in a 3v2 rondo that starts with a deliberate back-pass. Make the keeper the trigger of the build-up, not the escape route at the end of it.
